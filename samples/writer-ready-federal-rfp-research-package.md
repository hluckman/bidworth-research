# Writer-Ready Federal RFP Research Package

**Purpose:** sanitized BidWorth Research sample showing the research-heavy front end that can be delivered before a proposal writer drafts the final narrative.

> This is a workflow/sample, not a claim of prior federal contract performance, award history, certification, or client work. For a live engagement, every solicitation fact, amendment, award datum, incumbent assertion, pricing datum, and FAR reference is re-verified against the controlling official source before delivery.

## 2. Section-to-compliance matrix

| RFP reference | Requirement | Response location | Evidence/input needed | Risk | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L/M/[section] | [verbatim-short requirement] | [planned volume/section] | [client evidence] | High/Med/Low | Open/Ready |

Extraction covers mandatory forms, page/format limits, representations, evaluation factors, technical requirements, staffing/key-personnel requirements, past-performance instructions, pricing schedules, certifications, and submission mechanics.

## 3. Bid/no-bid gates

Before recommending pursuit, test independently:

- entity and registration eligibility;
- set-aside / socioeconomic eligibility;
- required licenses, clearances, certifications, facilities, geography, or security conditions;
- minimum past-performance or key-personnel thresholds;
- ability to satisfy mandatory technical requirements;
- schedule and delivery feasibility;
- required bonding/insurance where applicable;
- availability of bidder-owned evidence for every material representation.

A failed mandatory gate is reported as a disqualifier, not papered over with proposal language.

## 4. FAR / clause risk register

| Clause / provision | Why it matters here | Required bidder action | Source | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [number] | [plain-English impact] | [document/decision/action] | [official clause/package] | Client/BidWorth |

BidWorth flags solicitation-specific clauses and translates their practical impact. Legal interpretation or a contractual commitment is escalated to the client/qualified counsel rather than guessed.

## 5. Award and incumbent research

For the relevant agency, requirement, NAICS/PSC and predecessor vehicle:

- identify source-documented prior awards and modifications;
- distinguish predecessor/incumbent evidence from similarly named contracts;
- capture awardee, vehicle, award date, ceiling/obligations only where the source supports it;
- note competition type and bidder count when publicly supported;
- separate verified facts from analytical inference.

Suggested sources: USASpending, FPDS-derived official records where available, SAM.gov notices, agency procurement forecasts and award notices.

## 6. Competitor positioning

Build an evidence table rather than unsupported competitor claims:

| Company | Evidence of relevance | Vehicle/capability evidence | Potential strength | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [name] | [source] | [source] | [fact-based observation] | High/Med/Low |

No claim about a competitor's private pricing, performance quality, staffing, or intended bid is presented as fact without support.

## 7. Pricing intelligence

Normalize only comparable evidence:

- historical awarded line items where public;
- contract ceiling vs. obligated amount (kept distinct);
- labor categories/rates where legitimately public;
- quantity, geography, period, escalation, and contract-type differences;
- current market benchmarks from traceable sources.

Output includes assumptions and comparability warnings so a proposal writer does not mistake a weak benchmark for an expected winning price.

## 8. Agency priorities / “hot buttons”

Translate documented agency priorities into proposal-useful themes using current strategic plans, acquisition forecasts, public program material, Q&A/amendments, and the solicitation's evaluation language. Each theme receives a source and confidence level; rhetoric is not invented and generic agency slogans are not treated as evaluation criteria.

## 9. Differentiator evidence map

A differentiator is only proposed when the bidder can substantiate it.

| Candidate differentiator | Bidder evidence required | Evaluation factor served | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| [claim] | [metric/reference/document] | [factor] | Verified/Pending/Reject |

Past performance belongs to the bidder. BidWorth does not invent projects, references, outcomes, contract wins, metrics, or customer endorsements.

## 10. Writer handoff

The final research package is organized so the proposal writer can move directly into drafting:

1. one-page pursuit summary and GO / CONDITIONAL / NO-GO recommendation;
2. controlled solicitation/amendment list;
3. compliance matrix;
4. risk/disqualifier register;
5. FAR/clause action register;
6. award/incumbent research with source trail;
7. competitor evidence table;
8. pricing-intelligence workbook/table;
9. agency-priority evidence map;
10. differentiator evidence map;
11. open client-input list ranked by deadline/criticality.

## Client inputs needed to execute a live package

Minimum intake: official solicitation link or number; bidder legal/entity facts relevant to eligibility; capabilities; registrations/certifications actually held; geography; past-performance material the bidder is authorized to use; key personnel facts where required; pricing constraints; and the desired decision/deliverable.

**BidWorth Research:** research, opportunity qualification, compliance mapping, procurement intelligence, and proposal-support drafting with source traceability and explicit verification boundaries.
