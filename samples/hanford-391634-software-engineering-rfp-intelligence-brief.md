## Immediate bidder workplan

### 1. Amendment and version control

Create a document register with columns for filename, revision/date, controlling/superseded status, affected requirement, and proposal section impacted. Amendment 01 should be read first and used to determine which original provisions or instructions it changes.

### 3. Bidder-fact intake

BidWorth can research and organize the solicitation, but the following facts must come from the bidder and must never be invented:

- legal entity name and small-business eligibility;
- active registrations/identifiers required by the RFP;
- actual software engineering personnel and labor categories;
- availability and place-of-performance facts;
- technical approach and tools the bidder can genuinely provide;
- past performance and corporate experience;
- labor rates, indirects, fee and final price;
- exceptions to commercial/supplemental provisions; and
- any certifications or representations requested in the package.

### 4. Technical response evidence map

For each SOW obligation, map the proposed response to a factual evidence source. Useful evidence may include named staff resumes, project records supplied by the bidder, tool/platform documentation, quality procedures, staffing plans, and client-authorized past-performance references. If the bidder cannot evidence a mandatory capability, flag it as a bid/no-bid or clarification issue rather than filling the gap with generic prose.

### 5. Pricing and labor controls

The presence of an SCA wage determination and ILOT materials makes labor mapping a material proposal risk. Before final pricing, reconcile every proposed labor category against the controlling solicitation instructions and wage/labor requirements. BidWorth can build the crosswalk and identify missing inputs; final rates, pricing strategy, legal classification judgments, and commercial commitments remain with the bidder.
